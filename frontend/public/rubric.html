01
Data Engineering & Visualization
08 / 08 Marks
🎯 What FailureDNA Implemented
  • Dataset: NASA C-MAPSS FD004 (249 train engines, 248 test engines, 41,214 cycles, 21 sensors, 3 settings).
  • Complexity: 6 distinct operating flight conditions + dual concurrent failure modes (HPC compressor and Fan degradation).
  • Zero-Leakage Normalization: RegimeAwarePreprocessor clusters conditions into 6 regimes and z-scores sensors strictly on initial healthy cycles (≤20 cycles).
  • Edge Padding: Window size W=50; left-edge padding with binary attention masks ($M_{win}$) for short engines (e.g. Unit #098 at 19 cycles).
  • Visualizations: 25+ figures in reports/figures/ (correlation matrices, 3D regime silhouettes, sensor trajectories).
🗣️ Viva Examiner Q&A
❓ Q: Why FD004 instead of FD001?
FD001 has only 1 flight condition and 1 failure mode (too simple). FD004 has 6 flight conditions and 2 failure modes, representing true industrial complexity.
❓ Q: How did you prevent data leakage?
We fit regime statistics only on cycles ≤ 20 of training engines. Test engines and late-stage degradation data are never seen during normalization fitting.

02
Advanced Modeling & Pipeline Optimization
10 / 10 Marks
🎯 What FailureDNA Implemented
  • Deep Learning Encoder: 4-Layer Pre-LayerNorm Temporal Transformer ($d_{model}=128, H=8, d_{ff}=256, \text{dropout}=0.1$).
  • Self-Supervised Objective: InfoNCE / NT-Xent loss (τ=0.07) with time-jitter and Gaussian jitter augmentations.
  • Signature Discovery: Spherical K-Means ($K=4$) assigns discrete degradation clusters (Nominal, Early, Accelerated, Critical).
  • Linear RUL Probe: Standardized Ridge Probe (α=10.0) with a standard 130-cycle piecewise linear degradation cap.
  • Decoupling Audit: Normalized Mutual Information ($\text{NMI} = 0.000143$) proves signatures track wear, not flight altitude.
  • Modular Pipeline: PyTorch FailureDNATrainer with AdamW, Cosine Annealing LR schedule, and early stopping.
🗣️ Viva Examiner Q&A
❓ Q: Why use a Transformer over LSTM?
Multi-head self-attention captures long-range dependencies across all 21 sensors simultaneously without vanishing gradients, and trains 3x faster via parallel sequence processing.
❓ Q: What does MaskedTemporalPooling do?
It averages sequence embeddings over valid flight cycles only, mathematically zeroing out dummy edge-padded cycles so they don't corrupt the engine health fingerprint.

Benchmarking & Comparative Analysis
10 / 10 Marks
🎯 What FailureDNA Implemented
  • Official Benchmark: Evaluated all models on all 248 official test engines of NASA C-MAPSS FD004.
  • Competitive Matrix: Compared against XGBoost, Random Forest, Multi-Layer Perceptrons, and Linear Ridge.
  • Metrics: Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), NASA Asymmetric Penalty Score ($S$), and $R^2$.
  • Hypothesis Testing: 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples (seed 42) + Holm-Bonferroni FWER step-down correction.
  • Key Finding: XGBoost has an MAE advantage (Δ = +2.86 cycles, p = 0.0150), but FailureDNA achieves a better NASA penalty score (5,607 vs 5,744) and discovers unsupervised health states.

🗣️ Viva Examiner Q&A
❓ Q: Did your Transformer beat XGBoost?
Honest, high-scoring answer: XGBoost achieved lower point MAE (18.79 vs 21.65), which we confirmed via 10,000 bootstrap tests. However, FailureDNA achieved a lower NASA asymmetric late-failure penalty (5,607 vs 5,744) and uniquely discovers 4 physical failure states without labels.
❓ Q: Why use the NASA Scoring Function?
Because in aviation, predicting late is catastrophic compared to predicting early. The NASA function exponentially penalizes late predictions: $s = \exp(d/10) - 1$.
